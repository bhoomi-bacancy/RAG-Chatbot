PATIENT CARE AND CLINICAL GUIDELINES

1. PAIN ASSESSMENT

Pain Scales
- Numeric Rating Scale (NRS): 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst imaginable pain). 1–3 mild, 4–6 moderate, 7–10 severe.
- Wong-Baker FACES Scale: used for children aged 3+ and patients who cannot verbalize pain.
- Visual Analogue Scale (VAS): horizontal line from no pain to worst pain.

WHO Analgesic Ladder (3-Step)
Step 1 (Mild pain): Non-opioids — Paracetamol, NSAIDs.
Step 2 (Moderate pain): Weak opioids — Codeine, Tramadol + non-opioids.
Step 3 (Severe pain): Strong opioids — Morphine, Fentanyl, Oxycodone + non-opioids.

2. INFECTION CONTROL

Standard Precautions
Apply to all patients regardless of diagnosis. Include hand hygiene, personal protective equipment (gloves, gowns, masks), safe handling of sharps, and respiratory hygiene.

Hand Hygiene — 5 Moments (WHO)
1. Before touching a patient
2. Before a clean/aseptic procedure
3. After body fluid exposure risk
4. After touching a patient
5. After touching patient surroundings

Use alcohol-based hand rub for visibly clean hands. Use soap and water when hands are visibly soiled or after caring for C. difficile patients.

Transmission-Based Precautions
- Contact precautions: MRSA, VRE, C. difficile, scabies.
- Droplet precautions: influenza, meningococcal disease, pertussis.
- Airborne precautions: tuberculosis, measles, chickenpox — negative pressure room.

3. WOUND CARE

Wound Assessment
Document: location, size (length × width × depth), wound bed, exudate, surrounding skin, odor, signs of infection (erythema, warmth, purulence, increased pain).

Wound Healing Phases
1. Hemostasis (minutes to hours): vasoconstriction, platelet aggregation, clot formation.
2. Inflammation (1–4 days): neutrophils and macrophages clean wound debris.
3. Proliferation (4–21 days): fibroblast activity, collagen deposition, angiogenesis, re-epithelialization.
4. Remodeling (21 days to 2 years): collagen reorganization, scar maturation.

Dressing Selection
- Non-adherent dressings: for clean healing wounds.
- Hydrocolloid: for low-to-moderate exudate wounds.
- Foam dressings: for moderate-to-heavy exudate.
- Silver-containing dressings: for infected or high infection-risk wounds.

4. NUTRITION IN PATIENT CARE

Malnutrition Screening
Use validated tools: MUST (Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool), NRS-2002, MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment).

MUST Scoring
- BMI score: >20 = 0, 18.5–20 = 1, <18.5 = 2.
- Unplanned weight loss: <5% = 0, 5–10% = 1, >10% = 2.
- Acute disease effect: if acutely ill with no nutritional intake likely for >5 days = 2.

Total score: 0 = low risk (routine care), 1 = medium risk (observe), ≥2 = high risk (refer to dietitian, treat).

Enteral Nutrition
Preferred over parenteral when gut is functional. Routes: nasogastric (NG) tube, nasojejunal, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG).

Parenteral Nutrition
Used when enteral route is not feasible. Central PN via central venous catheter for solutions >900 mOsm/L.

5. FALL PREVENTION

Risk Factors
Age >65, history of falls, medications (sedatives, opioids, antihypertensives, diuretics), cognitive impairment, incontinence, mobility impairment, vision problems.

Morse Fall Scale
Scores fall history, secondary diagnosis, ambulatory aid, IV/heparin access, gait, and mental status. Score ≥45 = high risk.

Prevention Interventions
- Bed rails up, bed in lowest position
- Non-slip footwear
- Call bell within reach
- Regular toileting schedules
- Environmental assessment (lighting, clutter)
- Medication review (reduce polypharmacy)
- Physiotherapy and exercise programs

6. EARLY WARNING SCORES

National Early Warning Score 2 (NEWS2)
Tracks: respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, supplemental oxygen use, systolic BP, pulse rate, level of consciousness, temperature.

Score interpretation:
- 0: Minimum 12-hourly monitoring.
- 1–4: Minimum 4–6-hourly monitoring.
- 5–6 or single extreme: Urgent review by ward-based doctor.
- 7+: Emergency assessment by critical care team, consider ICU transfer.

SpO2 Targets
- General patients: 94–98%
- COPD and hypercapnic respiratory failure: 88–92%
