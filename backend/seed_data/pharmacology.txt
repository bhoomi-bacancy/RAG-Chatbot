PHARMACOLOGY AND DRUG REFERENCE GUIDE

1. ANTIBIOTIC CLASSES

Penicillins
Mechanism: Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins.
Examples: Amoxicillin, Ampicillin, Flucloxacillin, Piperacillin-Tazobactam.
Uses: Respiratory infections, UTI, skin infections, endocarditis.
Side effects: Allergic reactions (rash, anaphylaxis), diarrhea, Clostridioides difficile colitis.

Cephalosporins (1st–5th Generation)
1st gen (Cephalexin): gram-positive coverage. 3rd gen (Ceftriaxone): gram-negative coverage, meningitis. 5th gen (Ceftaroline): MRSA coverage.
Side effects: Cross-hypersensitivity with penicillins (~1–2%), GI upset.

Macrolides (e.g., Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Erythromycin)
Mechanism: Inhibit bacterial protein synthesis (50S ribosomal subunit).
Uses: Atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia), community-acquired pneumonia, H. pylori eradication.
Side effects: QT prolongation, GI upset, hepatotoxicity.

Fluoroquinolones (e.g., Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin)
Mechanism: Inhibit DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV.
Uses: UTI, respiratory infections, traveler's diarrhea, certain STIs.
Cautions: Tendinopathy/tendon rupture, QT prolongation, avoid in pregnancy, children, myasthenia gravis.

Metronidazole
Mechanism: Disrupts DNA synthesis in anaerobic organisms and protozoa.
Uses: Anaerobic infections, C. difficile, Trichomonas, bacterial vaginosis, H. pylori (with PPI + amoxicillin).
Side effects: Metallic taste, nausea, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.

2. ANALGESICS

Opioids (e.g., Morphine, Codeine, Tramadol, Oxycodone, Fentanyl)
Mechanism: Agonists at μ, κ, δ opioid receptors in CNS and peripheral nervous system.
Uses: Moderate to severe pain, palliative care.
Side effects: Constipation, nausea, sedation, respiratory depression, dependence.
Reversal agent: Naloxone (opioid antagonist).

NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac, Ketorolac)
Mechanism: Inhibit COX-1 and COX-2, reducing prostaglandin synthesis.
Uses: Mild to moderate pain, fever, inflammation.
Contraindications: Peptic ulcer disease, renal impairment, third trimester pregnancy, severe heart failure.
Side effects: GI ulceration, renal impairment, platelet dysfunction.

3. CARDIOVASCULAR DRUGS

Diuretics
- Furosemide (loop diuretic): potent, used in heart failure and edema. Monitor electrolytes, especially potassium.
- Spironolactone (potassium-sparing): used in heart failure and hyperaldosteronism. Risk of hyperkalemia.
- Hydrochlorothiazide (thiazide): used in hypertension. Can cause hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hyperuricemia.

Anticoagulants
- Warfarin: vitamin K antagonist, requires INR monitoring (target 2–3 for AF). Multiple drug and food interactions.
- Heparin: unfractionated or LMWH. Used in acute settings. Reversed by protamine sulfate.
- DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, Edoxaban): fixed dose, no routine monitoring. Rivaroxaban reversal: Andexanet alfa. Dabigatran reversal: Idarucizumab.

4. ENDOCRINE DRUGS

Insulin Types
- Rapid-acting (Aspart, Lispro, Glulisine): onset 10–15 min, peak 1–2 hrs, duration 3–5 hrs.
- Short-acting (Regular): onset 30–60 min, peak 2–4 hrs, duration 5–8 hrs.
- Intermediate (NPH): onset 1–3 hrs, peak 6–12 hrs, duration 16–24 hrs.
- Long-acting (Glargine, Detemir, Degludec): once daily basal, no pronounced peak.

Thyroid Medications
- Levothyroxine (T4): synthetic thyroid hormone for hypothyroidism. Take on empty stomach. TSH monitoring every 6–12 months.
- Carbimazole / Propylthiouracil (PTU): antithyroid drugs for hyperthyroidism. Risk of agranulocytosis.

5. DRUG INTERACTIONS - IMPORTANT EXAMPLES

- Warfarin + NSAIDs: increased bleeding risk
- SSRIs + Tramadol: serotonin syndrome risk
- Statins + Macrolides/Azole antifungals: increased risk of myopathy/rhabdomyolysis
- ACE Inhibitors + Potassium-sparing diuretics: hyperkalemia
- Metronidazole + Alcohol: disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, tachycardia, nausea)
- Fluoroquinolones + Antacids (containing Mg/Al/Ca): reduced absorption of quinolone

6. DRUG SAFETY IN SPECIAL POPULATIONS

Pregnancy (Teratogenic drugs to avoid)
- Category X: Thalidomide, Isotretinoin, Warfarin (1st trimester), Methotrexate, Statins.
- Safe in pregnancy: Paracetamol, Penicillins, Cephalosporins (most), Low-dose Aspirin (2nd trimester).

Renal Impairment
- Renally cleared drugs require dose reduction: Metformin (contraindicated if eGFR <30), Digoxin, Aminoglycosides, Lithium.
- Use with caution: NSAIDs (reduce renal blood flow), ACE inhibitors (monitor creatinine and potassium).

Hepatic Impairment
- Drugs with significant hepatic metabolism need dose reduction: Paracetamol (max 2g/day), Warfarin, Opioids, Statins.
- Avoid hepatotoxic drugs: Isoniazid, Valproate, Ketoconazole.
